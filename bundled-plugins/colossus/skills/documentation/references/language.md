# Language and tone

Write for the reader's technical knowledge and goal. Use a helpful, direct,
conversational tone without slang, hype, jokes, or claims that a task is easy.
Address the reader as "you" where natural. Use "we" only when its meaning is clear.

Prefer active voice and present tense when they make the actor and behavior clear.
Use short, concrete words and explicit subjects. Keep conditions near the action
and explain a new abbreviation when the intended audience needs it. Remove redundant
phrases without removing necessary technical detail.

Use terminology consistently. Distinguish what a feature does from what the reader
must do. Use American spelling and serial commas unless the user or repository
requires another convention. Prefer literal phrasing that is easy to translate;
avoid idioms, culturally specific metaphors, and ambiguous pronouns.

Keep literal API identifiers and established product names exact, even when their
spelling differs from ordinary prose. A general style recommendation does not
justify changing a public name or a repository's required terminology.
